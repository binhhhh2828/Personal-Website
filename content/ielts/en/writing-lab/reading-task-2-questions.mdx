Many essays lose Task Response marks not for a lack of ideas, but because they answer a different question from the one asked. The first step of thinking in writing is to **read the question like an analyst**.

## The three layers of a question

1. **Topic** — the broad field: education, environment, technology.
2. **Scope** — the specific limits within that topic: who, where, when.
3. **Task** — what you must do: give an opinion, discuss both views, analyse causes and solutions…

<EssayPrompt task={2}>
Some people believe that university students should be required to study subjects outside their main field. To what extent do you agree or disagree?
</EssayPrompt>

*Example question written for this lesson.*

| Layer | In the question above |
| --- | --- |
| Topic | Higher education |
| Scope | University students, courses **outside** their major, **compulsory** |
| Task | Extent of agreement — one clear position held throughout |

## Words that are easy to miss

Words such as *required*, *only*, *always* and *main* narrow the argument. Skip them and the essay drifts off-topic even when it still sounds reasonable.

<Compare
  before="Studying many subjects is good for students."
  after="Making non-major courses compulsory broadens graduates' thinking, but only if those courses are chosen with care."
  note="The second sentence keeps both key words: compulsory and non-major."
/>

## Self-check questions

- Can I restate the task in one sentence?
- Does my position answer the *degree* of agreement the question asks for?
